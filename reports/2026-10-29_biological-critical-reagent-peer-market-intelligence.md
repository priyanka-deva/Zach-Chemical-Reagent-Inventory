Manufacturing decision significance
The market decision is an operating-model question as well as a software question: can a laboratory demonstrate
that a specific lot is approved for a defined assay and available at the correct location, while preserving controlled
records and linking use to QC testing? Enterprise material stock alone cannot establish assay-specific authorization.
[G01,G02,T01,T04,R03]

### 1.2 — Main conclusion
Within the 19 requested peers, the reviewed sources yield four named but differently scoped platform cases (J&J,
Roche, AstraZeneca and Moderna), one explicit centralized critical-reagent practice without a named platform
(Pfizer), three operational-role disclosures (Gilead, Sanofi and Regeneron), and eleven without comparable
documented BCR software. These are evidence classifications, not adoption rates. Peer documentation supports
four technical operating approaches: a dedicated internal critical-reagent application; centralized practice with
undisclosed software; broader laboratory/scientific platforms; and logistics/procurement-oriented inventory. The
evidence is not sufficient to determine the most prevalent solution among the requested peers. [P01–P11,R01,R02]

### 4.1 — Comparative assessment criteria
For manufacturing use, documentation should show whether a product records: (1) scientific identity and method
applicability; (2) reagent lot genealogy and approval status; (3) aliquot/parent relationships and environmental
excursions; (4) retest and qualification evidence, including bridging; (5) laboratory test and instrument usage; (6)
lifecycle changes and audit records; (7) inventory ownership across internal sites and CROs; and (8) interfaces to
purchasing and material stock. These criteria are derived from regulatory controls and published
implementations. [G01,G02,R01–R04,T01,T07]
### 4.2 — Boundary between customer adoption and vendor functionality
Evidence that a vendor supports a feature is different from evidence that a customer has licensed, configured,
validated and placed that feature into operation. Similarly, a vendor’s named logo set establishes a commercial
association but not the exact product/module, operating site, assay domain or present deployment status.
[P01,P11,T01–T04]

### 6.1 — Implications for provider selection
Published research suggests that the distinguishing capability is the linkage of qualified reagent lot to the
applicable analytical method, usage context and scientific supporting evidence, with controlled change and
transfer events. Enhancements in location sensing or AI-based search are complementary to, not replacements for,
controlled assay authorization. [G01,R02–R04,D02,D03]

### 8.1 — Material-class boundaries
A commercial therapeutic, an analytical reference standard, an assay-critical binding reagent and a GMP
manufacturing raw material are distinct inventory objects with different intended uses and controls. The biological
nature of a material does not itself establish criticality; the relevant analytical method and use determine that
assessment. [G01,G02]

### 9.1 — Evidence still required for a peer-comparable deployment benchmark
A defensible next-stage benchmark would request publicly releasable customer deployment disclosures
identifying: named platform and module; laboratories/sites in scope; biological critical-reagent workflows covered;
qualification and bridging records; integrations with ERP, LIMS and CROs; and live versus planned status. The
source set here does not support a market-share percentage or 2026 site-by-site roll-out map. [P01–P11,T02]
### 9.2 — Executive conclusion
Conclusion
Public peer disclosures and primary product documentation support a multi-system market: laboratory
platforms, specialist critical-reagent systems, scientific inventory tools and procurement/materials systems can
each play a role. Genentech and Pfizer provide the clearest direct public descriptions of critical-reagent lifecycle
approaches; J&J, Roche, AstraZeneca and Moderna supply named but differently scoped technology evidence. A
company-level “which software is used for BCR inventory?” answer remains unverified for most peers. [P01–
P11,R01,R02]

as a distinct Roche subsidiary example. The search examined named case studies, product documentation,
technical help, company disclosures, conference abstracts, scientific papers and public job descriptions. Public
search absence is never interpreted as absence of an internal platform.
